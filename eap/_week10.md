## Week 10-3

**Argument Essay: Rural Living vs. Urban Living**

People continuously change their living environment between urban and rural
areas (Urban and rural migration, n.d.).
However, there comes the dilemma of whether to live in town or the country as
confused residents try to balance the easy access to facilities in a city with
the space and nature of rural areas.
It is crucial to make the right choice to get the most suitable lifestyle.
This essay will argue that living in a populated town or city is a better
choice than country life from the economy, safety, and public service
perspectives.

People tempted by the bucolic ideal of rural life tend to believe that economy
is a powerful motive for people to flee to the countryside.
Hamm (2013) states that Living standards in Iowa's capital city with an over
$52K annual income is equivalent to Boston's $85K, and life in a metropolitan
like Boston, it is argued, tends to be of a higher price.
This opinion, though convinced by many people, does not take into account the
plenty of job opportunities available only in urban areas.
Tech giants such as Google and Facebook, which always readily welcome software
engineers with considerable salaries, can only provide posts within
metropolitans rather than some rural areas.
For the more popular jobs, towns can be even more generous by providing urban
residents countless working opportunities, such as servicemen or truck drivers,
as populated areas can feed large numbers of customers to the service industry.
For rural dwellers, unfortunately, the chance to land a job with a sufficient
income is slim.
This means that starting a career could be far easier if one moves to a town or city.

Many people may believe that city life is riskier than the kind of countryside.
However, it is quite the opposite, as Steenhuysen (2013) argues that urbanites
are less likely to die from injuries than their country counterparts.
The adequacy of medical resources in town, such as hospitals and first aid
kits, means that patients in a city have more chances of being hospitalized in
time, while in rural regions, people sustaining severe injury may spend more
time being transported to a trauma center (Steenhuysen, 2013).

An Urban area usually enjoys a more improved transportation facility.
Not only the ubiquitous vehicles of public transport, such as the rapid
transmit or metros, which can carry hordes of passengers from the airport to
the city center and back again, but also customized digital transportation
platforms like Uber, are there outside of one's apartment, or even more
conveniently, at a customer's fingertip.
On the other hand, rural residents, if they want to go anywhere in time, must
own a car and pay for the maintenance and fuel cost (Hamm, 2013).

In conclusion, this essay has argued that living in urban areas is a more
suitable form of lifestyle considering the adequate job opportunities a city
could provide and the medical and public transport facilities.
Understandably, the rural fantasy always appeals to hopeful city dwellers tired
of the stress in the city, but as long as they can cope with fast-paced urban
life, the urbanite can surely enjoy a safer, healthier, and more convenient
life.
